# L03-M01 - Volume Measures Activity, Not Direction

**Estimated time:** 28 minutes

## Objective
Explain what chart volume measures and avoid treating a large volume bar as a directional signal by itself.

## Why it matters
Volume shows how much trading activity occurred during a period, but the bar alone does not explain intent or guarantee what price will do next.

## Core ideas
- Stock volume is commonly expressed as shares traded, while futures volume is expressed as contracts traded.
- Interpret volume with price location, structure, session, and the instrument's data source.
- High activity can accompany continuation, reversal, liquidation, or two-sided balance.
- Separate the observed fact from the story used to explain it.

## Scenario
US100 prints its highest five-minute volume bar of the session at a prior range high, but price closes back inside the range. The activity is clear; direction still requires context.

## Practice
Select five unusually large volume bars. Record only the observable price response before writing any interpretation.

## Knowledge check
- **What does a volume bar measure?** Trading activity during the selected period.
- **Does high volume prove buyers are in control?** No.
- **What should accompany a volume reading?** Price, structure, session, and data-source context.

## Common mistake
Calling every high-volume bar bullish or bearish without examining the price response.

## Risk note
Activity alone is not an entry signal. Define invalidation and risk before any trade decision.

## Evidence classification
Platform documentation and professional observation
