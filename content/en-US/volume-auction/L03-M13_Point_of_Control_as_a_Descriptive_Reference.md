# L03-M13 - Point of Control as a Descriptive Reference

**Estimated time:** 38 minutes

## Objective
Identify the Point of Control for a defined volume-profile window and use it as a descriptive reference without assuming that price must return to, hold, or reject it.

## Why it matters
The Point of Control highlights the profile row with the greatest recorded activity in the selected sample. It can help organize context, but its location depends on the data and calculation settings and does not forecast the next price move.

## Core ideas
- The Point of Control, or POC, is the price row with the highest recorded activity in a defined volume-profile window under the platform's calculation method.
- Changing the symbol, provider, volume type, window, session, row size, or lower-timeframe data can move the displayed POC.
- A POC records where activity concentrated in the selected sample. It does not identify participant intent or prove fair value.
- Price trading above, below, through, or near a prior POC is descriptive until structure, current participation, and price response provide additional evidence.
- A POC is not guaranteed support, resistance, a magnet, an entry, a stop, or a target. Unclear data or conflicting context supports a no-trade decision.

## Scenario
A completed Gold session has a POC near the middle of its range. The next session opens above it and later rotates toward it. Record the approach and subsequent price response without assuming that the POC attracted price or must act as support. Require current participation, structural context, and predefined invalidation before considering a plan.

## Practice
Mark the POC on three completed session profiles of the same instrument using consistent data, session, and calculation settings. Document the provider, volume type, window, row settings, and lower-timeframe input. Describe each later interaction using only observable facts, then finish with either a conditional, risk-defined hypothesis or a justified no-trade conclusion.

## Knowledge check
- **What does a volume-profile POC identify?** The price row with the highest recorded activity in the defined sample under the selected calculation settings.
- **Can changing the profile window or row settings move the POC?** Yes.
- **Does a prior POC guarantee support, resistance, or a return to that price?** No.

## Common mistake
Treating the POC as proven fair value or as a guaranteed price magnet, support, resistance, entry, stop, or target.

## Risk note
Do not derive a trade from POC location alone. Verify the data and profile settings, require current price and participation evidence, define invalidation and controlled risk, and stand aside when the reference conflicts with structure or the comparison is unclear.

## Evidence classification
Official platform calculation documentation
