# L02-M04 - Support and Resistance Zones

**Estimated time:** 30 minutes

## Objective
Draw support and resistance as decision zones rather than fragile exact lines.

## Why it matters
Real markets do not always respect a single pixel-perfect price. Zones help learners account for spread, volatility, liquidity sweeps, and different market venues.

## Core ideas
- Support is an area where demand previously overcame supply strongly enough to rotate price higher.
- Resistance is an area where supply previously overcame demand strongly enough to rotate price lower.
- Zones are more practical than exact lines when volatility is meaningful.
- A level is stronger when it aligns with structure, session context, and visible reaction.

## Scenario
Gold reacts three times around a 10-dollar band, not a single exact tick. Treating the level as one precise price creates unnecessary stop-outs.

## Practice
Replace three exact horizontal lines on a chart with zones. Explain what evidence justifies each zone.

## Knowledge check
- **Why use zones?**
  - Because markets often react around areas, not exact prices.
- **What gives a zone more value?**
  - Repeated structural reaction and context.
- **Should zones become excuses for wide risk?**
  - No.

## Common mistake
Making zones so wide that they no longer support a clear risk decision.

## Evidence classification
Widely accepted professional practice
