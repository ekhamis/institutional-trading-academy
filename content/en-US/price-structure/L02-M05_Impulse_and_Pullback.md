# L02-M05 - Impulse and Pullback

**Estimated time:** 30 minutes

## Objective
Distinguish directional expansion from corrective movement.

## Why it matters
A trader who enters during exhaustion often buys after the opportunity has already moved. Understanding impulse and pullback helps students wait for better location.

## Core ideas
- An impulse is a stronger directional movement that expands price away from a prior area.
- A pullback is a counter-move that tests whether the trend side can defend structure.
- Healthy pullbacks are usually smaller and less aggressive than the prior impulse.
- Deep or violent pullbacks warn that the trend may be losing quality.

## Scenario
DAX40 breaks higher at the London open, then retraces into a prior resistance zone. The question is whether that pullback is controlled or whether sellers have taken control.

## Practice
On a trending chart, label three impulses and three pullbacks. Note which pullback was healthiest and why.

## Knowledge check
- **What is an impulse?**
  - A stronger directional expansion.
- **What does a pullback test?**
  - Whether the trend side can defend structure.
- **What warns of trend weakness?**
  - A deep, aggressive, or disorderly pullback.

## Common mistake
Chasing the impulse after reward-to-risk has already deteriorated.

## Evidence classification
Widely accepted professional practice
