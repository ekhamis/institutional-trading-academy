# L02-M11 - Candlestick Context

**Estimated time:** 30 minutes

## Objective
Read candles inside market context instead of treating single candles as standalone signals.

## Why it matters
A candle does not mean the same thing in every location. A strong bullish candle at range resistance may be late buying, while the same candle after a controlled pullback may show trend continuation.

## Core ideas
- Candles summarize open, high, low, and close for a selected period.
- A candle's meaning depends on location, prior structure, volatility, and session timing.
- Single-candle patterns are weaker without context.
- Professional reading asks what the candle changed, rejected, confirmed, or failed to do.

## Scenario
A bullish candle appears on US100. On one chart it breaks from a clean pullback; on another it closes directly into higher-timeframe resistance. Same candle body, different decision quality.

## Practice
Find three large bullish candles. Label whether each is continuation, exhaustion, breakout, or unclear based on context.

## Knowledge check
- **Does a candle have the same meaning everywhere?**
  - No.
- **What gives a candle meaning?**
  - Location, structure, volatility, and timing.
- **Should a single candle replace a trade plan?**
  - No.

## Common mistake
Trading memorized candle patterns without context.

## Evidence classification
Widely accepted professional practice
