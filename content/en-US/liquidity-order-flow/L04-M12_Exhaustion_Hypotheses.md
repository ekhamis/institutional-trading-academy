# L04-M12 - Exhaustion Hypotheses

## Objective
Evaluate exhaustion hypotheses using reproducible observations and a conditional risk decision.

## Worked synthetic example
A push trades 300 contracts across six ticks; the next equal-duration push trades 80 across one tick.

## Interpretation and alternatives
- Declining participation and progress can support an exhaustion hypothesis. A quiet period or incomplete feed can produce similar observations.
- Absorption emphasizes opposing liquidity against aggressive trades; exhaustion emphasizes declining aggressive activity. They can coexist.
- Reject the hypothesis if participation and directional progress return; avoid inferring direction from low volume alone.
- All numeric examples are synthetic and must not be interpreted as historical performance.

## Practice
Recalculate the worked case, state one alternative explanation, and submit a decision-time record with data settings, confirmation, invalidation, costs and a justified no-trade condition.

## Answer rationale
- What does the worked case establish? Declining participation and progress can support an exhaustion hypothesis. A quiet period or incomplete feed can produce similar observations.
- What cancels or limits the interpretation? Reject the hypothesis if participation and directional progress return; avoid inferring direction from low volume alone.
- Does this example establish a profitable strategy? No. It is a synthetic reasoning exercise; an edge requires separate cost-aware evaluation.
