# L04-M05 - Sweeps, Breaks, and Post-Event Evidence

**Estimated time:** 42 minutes

## Objective
Classify a move through a reference level without assuming manipulation, then evaluate acceptance, rejection, execution conditions, and invalidation after the event.

## Why it matters
A brief move beyond a prior high or low can be called a sweep, stop run, breakout, or failed break. The label does not explain cause or determine the next direction.

## Core ideas
- Describe the observable event first: price traded beyond a defined reference, available depth changed, trades occurred, and price either remained beyond or returned.
- A market order prioritizes execution, not a guaranteed price. When liquidity is thin or orders are triggered together, fills can occur across several prices.
- Post-event acceptance requires evidence over time, such as sustained trading beyond the level, compatible structure, participation, and a clear invalidation. A single wick is insufficient.
- Rejection is also contextual. A return inside a range can fail again, and neither rejection nor acceptance proves participant intent.
- Use the event to update a scenario, not to manufacture certainty. Late confirmation, excessive stop distance, or conflicting data can require no trade.

## Scenario
Gold trades above a prior session high during a data release, fills across several prices, and closes back below. The move is observable; whether it was a stop run, failed breakout, or temporary volatility is interpretive. Assess the next completed evidence and the required risk.

## Practice
Review ten moves through prior highs or lows. Record the reference, spread and depth conditions, executed response, time spent beyond the level, structural outcome, and whether a controlled plan existed after confirmation.

## Knowledge check
- **Does trading beyond a prior high prove manipulation?** No. The event is observable, but cause and intent require evidence not supplied by the price path alone.
- **What does a market order guarantee?** It prioritizes execution, but it does not guarantee the execution price.
- **When should a post-sweep idea be rejected?** When confirmation is absent or late, structure invalidates, data conflicts, or required risk is excessive.

## Common mistake
Buying or selling automatically after any wick through a prior level and explaining the move with unobservable participant intent.

## Risk note
Triggered orders and thin depth can cause slippage. Do not place invalidation where normal event volatility can consume the full risk without confirming the thesis.

## Evidence classification
Official execution, volume-profile context, and exchange risk education
