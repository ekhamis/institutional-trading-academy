# Decision Quality Practice

Status: draft learning resource. All numbers and scenarios are synthetic, not
historical trades or recommendations. Use a simulator; no result establishes an
edge. English is the canonical edition.

## Before You Begin

For each scenario, write your decision before reading the review. Record the
instrument and feed you would need to verify, the evidence for and against the
idea, an invalidation, maximum loss including estimated costs, and a reason not
to trade. A chart label or another trader's reported result is not execution
evidence. Stops may fill worse than expected, particularly around news.

## Case 1: Waiting Is a Decision

An index breaks above a morning range just before a scheduled economic release.
Your delayed chart shows the break, but you cannot verify the current spread.
You have no confirmed live feed or preplanned event-risk rule.

**Question:** Is it possible to calculate and control the intended risk reliably?
What would you do and what information would change your decision?

**Review:** Stand aside while the feed and spread are unknown. A delayed chart
cannot establish an executable price. Verify the feed, event time, instrument,
spread and your risk rules before considering a later setup. No entry is a valid
outcome; waiting does not guarantee a better opportunity.

## Case 2: A Good Plan Can Lose

On a hypothetical instrument, a simulated long entry is 100, its planned stop
is 98, and its intended target is 104. The instrument is worth $1 per point per
unit. Assume two units and a total round-trip cost of $1, with no slippage.
The stop is filled at 98. The student recorded a conditional trigger and a
contradictory higher-timeframe signal before entering.

**Question:** Calculate the realized loss and assess the process separately
from the result. What if the stop instead filled at 97.5?

**Review:** At 98 the loss is (100 - 98) x 2 x $1 + $1 = $5. At 97.5 it is
$6, demonstrating slippage. The planned gross reward was $8 and gross risk
$4; costs reduce the net target profit to $7 in this simplified example. A
loss alone does not prove the plan was bad. Assess whether the stated trigger,
size and execution followed the plan, and do not assume missing details.

## Case 3: Outcome Is Not Permission

A simulated trader skips the maximum-loss calculation, doubles the position
after entry, and later reports a large gain. The screenshot has no fills or
complete order history.

**Question:** Should the learner pass the risk assessment? What is known?

**Review:** No. The absent risk calculation is a mandatory remediation item.
The gain is an unverified report, and an outcome cannot validate an unsafe
process. Request a decision-time plan and independently supported execution
records; do not infer the missing stop, costs or risk from the screenshot.

## Reflection Checklist

- Did I distinguish observation, interpretation and a claim to test?
- Did I document both supporting and contradictory evidence before the result?
- Could I reproduce the risk arithmetic with the instrument's actual terms?
- Did I record a skipped setup and state what remained unknown?
- Would I make the same process assessment if the result had been reversed?
